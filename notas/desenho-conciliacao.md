## 1. The design in one page

**What does not move.** `runAllocationEngine(state)` and its goldens are untouched by every step. The only golden changes
are two optional commits that need Pedro's explicit yes ("go with defaults" does not cover them): C2 (the start rule of the
INVA ranges, 37 of 41 files) and C's "texto" (7 lines of `cenario-06.json`). `gravarAlteracao` stays the only writer;
`dia.pedidos` stays the engine's only input, so pins, statuses, results, the nine History revert kinds, the TV, the BI and
the exports keep working on today's keys (`<stuId>_<mid>_1`).

**Pedidos (D1, D2, D3).** `solicitacoes` gains an approval state and two preference columns (eight nullable columns, one
migration); Operador and Administrador approve and withdraw; an Operador's own pedido is born approved ("Registrar e
aprovar"); the deadline, the Exceção da Coordenação (now refused on Sundays and past dates) and the local-flight limit stay;
the page is F2's list of the open date, with the form ("Data do pedido") in the detail pane; one cache of the date's
pedidos, read again every 60 s with the day's version. Module and table names stay (`agenda`, `solicitacoes`).

**Processamento (E, G1, G2).** `js/processamento.js` (pure) conciliates the date's pedidos with the day: entries, returns
and exits under guards (`ficaNaEscala`), the course mission bound at entry by time (`missaoDaEntrada`, mark
`etapaDoPrograma`). It is applied by one explicit, logged write, "Atualizar a escala", and at once after an approval only on
a date already started (D's five conditions). `js/rejeicoes.js` pairs the engine's rejections with requests by position;
`js/restricoes.js` writes the operator restrictions on the record. The screen `#s-proc` shows each pedido enriched and why
it cannot be scheduled ("não cabe nem sozinho", "não coube na escala"). A flight's restrictions (instrutor, prioridade, tipo
de recurso, duração, horário, missão do curso, "Tirar da escala") are edited in G1's drawer "Restrições do voo", opened from
the Escala and, from G2, from Processamento.

**Escala and Aprovar (B, H).** The Escala keeps its four views and every flight action; H gives it an inputs row
`#entradas` (restrições do operador, pedidos processados, critérios; the option labels "Avião" and "Não mudar" stay). B moves
the per-date closing of periods from Recursos to `details#periodos` on the Escala (`win` and the engine's reads stay). H's
`#s-apr`: the pending items as links, approve and reopen (approving also pins what the approval lets in), the three exports
with a record, "Compartilhar".

**Cadastros (F3, F2, A, A2, B, C).** One cadastro flag `inativo` (effective from today on; the engine reads
`estadoParaEscalar`) and `exige` (ids of the habilitações registry) in format 26; F2's list–detail component lands with
Alunos; A adds nome completo, missão anterior and atual, results without a flight, and the Secretaria stops editing personal
data (M1 in A, M2 in A2); B rebuilds Recursos by category with the hangar order and the take-off times placed; C rebuilds
INVAs with per-date ranges `faixas` (format 27, today's presence meaning), periods and turnos as one-click chips, and deletes
the grid and both record drawers.

**The shell (F1a, F1b).** Four stage cards in `nav#linha`, a secondary line of links (Cadastros, Rodízio, BI); the rail
`aside#trilho` with the month calendar as the only control of the open date (docked from 1,280 px; the cap moves from 1,280
to 1,612 px) and the date's notifications; screens in the address `#/<tela>[/<item>]`. Record drawers go; the Histórico, the
Sugestões, the four dialogs and the map pop-up stay.

**Delivery.** plan → F0a, F0b, F0c (no visible change: test drivers, code seams, one stylesheet per owner) → F3 → F1a → F1b
→ F2 → four parallel lines [A → A2 → D1 → D2 → D3], [B], [C], [E's pure module], rejoined in the order A, A2, B, C, D1, D2,
D3, E → G1 → G2 → H → I; C2 alone, only with a yes. 19 PRs (20 with C2), five sittings (Onda 0 to 4), three sweeps of the
stored days (formats 26, 27, 28), five migration files.

**How solid.** Strong where it was measured: the conciliation (two property runs of 24,000 steps), the golden-neutral
reading of ranges (5,345,280 interval cases), the rejection pairing (9,040 rejections, 0 out of line), the approval pinning
(641 states), the export fingerprint, the list's redraw while a control is in use (Chromium runs). Weak where nothing ran:
every layout number is computed, no e2e of any step was run, the server counts (Secretaria accounts, stored formats, closed
periods, partial INVA days) were not taken, and the operator's screen width is unknown. The eight notes disagreed on about
fifty points, mostly names, owners and two crossed revisions (each of two notes adopted the other's first version); section
2 settles them all except what section 7 lists. The design's three real risks: the start-versus-presence rule (the request's
wording, declined by default with evidence), G2's cut-over (rehearsed on the preview; with 28 placed in E, G2 can be
reverted alone), and F2 as the single dependency of the parallel phase (it grows here by four small interface additions).

**The operator's routine** (entrega 2.9, re-counted with the rail docked from 1,280 px). A typical date costs 33
interactions today and 42 after H (43 with the rail undocked): +1 per restriction edit (open the flight, then the field),
+4 for a student with no pedido after 13:00 (the exception), +2 for the trip to Aprovar and back, +1 for the INVAs' pass, +1
the first time a period is closed on the Escala. In return: late pedidos on a started date enter by themselves (−2 per
batch), a cancelled pedido's flight leaves by itself (−1 and nothing forgotten), stale course missions are fixed for every
student by one click, every restriction of the date is in one list, and news from another computer arrives within 60 s.
